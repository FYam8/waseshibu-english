The 151 routes with unequal focus tags are not 151 misroutes: annual tags and skill subtypes differ legitimately. Source text review confirmed a narrower issue in the mixed context pool. FY2019 5-2 asks for a dialogue utterance that fits the following “I have got all I want”; FY2020 5-2 asks for an alternative car proposal fitting the following faster/safe canoe response. FY2019 5-3 asks for the emotion word disappointed after finding nothing in the mailbox. All three previously used a shared dialogue/lexical/sentence pool.

New cycles use six dialogue families (lcx01/02/06/09 and rdt_cx01/04), or five lexical-in-context families (lcx03/07/10 and rdt_cx02/05). Prompts and alternatives were reread: dialogue tasks infer the next utterance from plans, corrections, an offer, advice or the next response; lexical tasks infer crowded/reliable/confusing/reluctant/hesitated from surrounding evidence. The latter is contextual vocabulary practice, not a claim that every question specifically tests disappointment. Existing item text and identifiers are retained.

Immediate training excludes the two reserved confirmation families. Reservations deliberately include one item outside the former context pool, allowing new routes to be distinguished from old reservations without changing state schemas. Old reservations, pending cycles and active questions keep the old route. A failed confirmation ends that cycle; its restarted training may adopt the reviewed route. Retired or missing reviewed items fail closed. No shared engine, mastery thresholds, old answers, scores or completed mastery is rewritten.

Scope: three source questions, ten existing context items and four detail-context items were read. This is same-model semantic review; it is not independent human adjudication, a complete review of all 283 active exercises, or a measured learning gain.
